### Changed

- **A catch-up that resolved a conflict of meaning reopens review of the merged head** (zheref/hatsu#583). `en` § 3 now says the merged head is a changed tree for review as well as for tests: before the readiness verdict reaches the maintainer, `hanten` runs each applicable reviewer's bounded delta pass from its last reviewed head to the merge commit, under the effort's remaining budget, on murasaki § 5's return, before kokusen, kotoamatsukami and byakugan (mukai: step 2 before steps 3–5). An earlier settled round carries over its dispositions on paths the merge left byte-identical, never the resolved hunks, a finding whose cited lines moved, or its verdict as a claim about the merged head; after a no-op, clean or mechanical catch-up the settled round carries over unchanged. Skipping the round is the maintainer's ruling only, recorded under the new human-gate row `merged-head-review-skip` (G5), and where they rule disclosure enough the merge prompt carries one fixed line. `en` § 5's waiver condition reads hanten as settled on the merged head after such a catch-up; `ao` § 6 and `murasaki` § 4 point to the rule.
- **An unavailable host merge has a named route** (zheref/hatsu#586). Murasaki never uses the host merge itself: § 3's `nen wc catch-up` is the route either way, and only an unavailable host merge's refusal triggers the protected-path read. `murasaki` § 4 derives the incoming diff with `git -C <path> diff --no-renames --name-only HEAD...origin/<base>`, so a rename is listed under both its old and new path, compares it with the host's quoted refusal, takes ao's ordinary `nen wc catch-up` when no incoming path is protected and stops at G5 naming each protected path otherwise. After the catch-up and before the push it reads the set the catch-up actually wrote (`HEAD^1 HEAD` for a merge, the pre-catch-up head for a rebase) and compares again, since ao's catch-up fetches for itself; a protected path not in the first list is the same G5, the merge left unpushed. A non-zero exit from the fetch or either diff, or an empty list on a branch behind its base, stops the route at G5 with git's stderr quoted. The G5 is the new human-gate row `host-merge-protected-path`. One report line quoting the refusal satisfies its "tell the maintainer" and stops nothing on its own; the report carries both lists. The protected set is the host's and not in the checkout, so the check stays a recorded judgement by the maintainer's ruling [HR-393](docs/ROSTER.md#hr-393); ROSTER 1.82.0.
- **Declined cleanup rows are remembered, and still offered** (zheref/hatsu#584). WORKFLOW § *Declined cleanup rows*: at close, every offered `plan[]` row the maintainer's answer did not pick is appended to `<core>/.nen/cleanup/declined.jsonl`, each line carrying `"contract": "hatsu.cleanup.declined/v0.1"`. A later inventory folds a row whose object, class and size match its latest line, under 30 days old, written by an earlier sitting, into one proposed `plan[]` row the maintainer can still tick, so HR-210's prior leftovers stay offered; a same-sitting re-offer shows its own declines in full, `for all` lists every row, and an unreadable file or line folds nothing. The 30 days is the lead's reading, borrowed from cleanup § 4's run-record age. The append and the fold are by-hand, owed with the inventory read (zheref/nen#409, PROCESS § *Residue and owned dependencies*). `cleanup` § 7 and its Residue, and Shizuku's definition, cite it.
- **`limbo` adopts nen's portable mirror form** (zheref/hatsu#578). Step 4 resolves the checkout with `nen canon checkout` in nen's own order (`--canon-checkout "$canon"` when passed, then the registry's `checkout_env`, then `checkout`; a present step that fails fails the run), passes `$canon` only after an `unresolvable` exit, and routes each exit; steps 5 and 10 write their flags out, `--stack-dir handbooks/stacks --leaf rules [--canon-checkout "$canon"]`. The `mkdir` before `--markdown-out` is gone. Residues 5 and 6 are retired, citing zheref/nen#292 and zheref/nen#294, and WORKFLOW's local verification gate step 2 and `docs/config/canon-values.yml.md` give the portable `iteration.canonMirror.command` argv.
- **`mugetsu` records the go as data and splits targets by `nonProduction`** (zheref/hatsu#579). The maintainer's go is recorded through `nen stop answer --gate G3 --row release-go --typed-file --repo <path>` once the destination and the tag resolve and before anything is sent (§ 2 step 4), its id beside § 7's verbatim quote; a re-run within the same run cites the existing record, and a record that cannot be written sends nothing, the go lapsing with the run. A repository declaring no production destination, with none named, is refused, naming `kagutsuchi`. `mugetsu` § 5b and `kagutsuchi` § 2 read `project.targets.<name>.nonProduction` failing closed, absent or `false` being production, and quote `why` without reading it as the flag; a `deploy.defaultTarget` is still honoured only where its `why` also reads non-production (HR-289). **Consumer effect**: a non-production target declared without `nonProduction: true` now reads as production for a typed send as well as a default, so `kagutsuchi` refuses it and a bare `mugetsu` counts it as a production destination, until the consumer adds the flag. Residues 1 and 5 are retired.
