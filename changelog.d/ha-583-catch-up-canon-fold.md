### Changed

- **A catch-up that resolved a conflict of meaning reopens review of the merged head** (zheref/hatsu#583). `en` § 3 now says the merged head is a changed tree for review as well as for tests: before the readiness verdict reaches the maintainer, `hanten` runs each applicable reviewer's bounded delta pass from its last reviewed head to the merge commit, under the effort's remaining budget, on murasaki § 5's return, before kokusen, kotoamatsukami and byakugan (mukai: step 2 before steps 3–5). An earlier settled round carries over its dispositions on paths the merge left byte-identical, never the resolved hunks, a finding whose cited lines moved, or its verdict as a claim about the merged head; after a no-op, clean or mechanical catch-up the settled round carries over unchanged. A merge the maintainer made by hand (row `host-merge-protected-path`, option A) is a third state: with no conflicted path it carries the settled round over; with a conflict resolved by hand it owes the delta round, the resolved paths named. Skipping the round is the maintainer's ruling only, recorded under the new human-gate row `merged-head-review-skip` (G5, typed by design, never offered; an idle run owes the round), and where they rule disclosure enough the merge prompt carries one fixed line. `en` § 5's waiver condition reads hanten as settled on the merged head after such a catch-up; `ao` § 6 and `murasaki` § 4 point to the rule.
- **An unavailable host merge has a named route** (zheref/hatsu#586). Murasaki never uses the host merge itself: § 3's `nen wc catch-up` is the route either way, and only an unavailable host merge's refusal triggers the protected-path read. `murasaki` § 4 derives the incoming diff with `git -C <path> diff --no-renames --name-only HEAD...origin/<base>`, so a rename is listed under both its old and new path, compares it with the host's quoted refusal, takes ao's ordinary `nen wc catch-up` when no incoming path is protected and stops at G5 naming each protected path otherwise. After the catch-up and before the push it reads the set the catch-up actually wrote with one form for every strategy, fast-forward included, `git -C <path> diff --no-renames --name-only <pre-catch-up head> HEAD` (the head noted by step 1's `rev-parse` and carried across a conflict stop; a `noOp` wrote nothing), and compares again, since ao's catch-up fetches for itself. A protected path not in the first list is the same G5, and the protected write has then already been committed locally: it is held unpushed, nothing pushes from the branch while the stop stands, and the stop names `git -C <path> reset --keep <pre-catch-up head>` as the return point, run only on the maintainer's pick. A non-zero exit from the `rev-parse`, the fetch, either diff or the `rev-list --count`, or an empty list on a branch behind its base, stops the route at G5 with git's stderr quoted. Because § 6's publish gate re-probes with a real `nen wc catch-up`, a merge it makes is read at once from the last head a comparison cleared, and a later pass takes that cleared head as its baseline, never a merged head no comparison read. The G5 is the new human-gate row `host-merge-protected-path`, whose option B splits a conflicted catch-up (ao's abort line) from a completed one (held unpushed, the reset line named). One report line quoting the refusal satisfies its "tell the maintainer" and stops nothing on its own; the report carries both lists. The protected set is the host's and not in the checkout, so the check stays a recorded judgement by the maintainer's ruling [HR-393](docs/ROSTER.md#hr-393); ROSTER 1.82.0.

The 30 days is the lead's reading, borrowed from cleanup § 4's run-record age. The append and the fold are by-hand, owed with the inventory read (zheref/nen#409, PROCESS § *Residue and owned dependencies*). `cleanup` § 7 and its Residue, and Shizuku's definition, cite it.

- **`mugetsu` records the go as data and splits targets by `nonProduction`** (zheref/hatsu#579). The maintainer's go is recorded through `nen stop answer --gate G3 --row release-go --typed-file --repo <path>` once the destination and the tag resolve and before anything is sent (§ 2 step 4), its id beside § 7's verbatim quote; a re-run within the same run cites the existing record, read from `nen stop answer show`'s text since it exits `0` with `no stop answers recorded`, and a record that cannot be written sends nothing, the go lapsing with the run.
